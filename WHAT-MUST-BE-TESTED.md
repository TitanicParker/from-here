# What must be tested

## Purpose

This page converts the record into questions capable of independent resolution. It deliberately avoids asking experts to accept the claimant's conclusions.

Every answer should identify:

1. the evidence available at the relevant time;
2. the applicable standard;
3. the reasonable range of responses;
4. the strongest contrary evidence;
5. whether a departure occurred; and
6. separately, whether that departure probably caused harm.

---

# A. July 2017 clinical questions

### 1. Initial treatment strategy

Was the decision to use exceptional caution with levodopa/dopamine replacement in a very young, atypical patient within a reasonable range of neurological practice in July 2017?

The answer must account for the dramatic early treatment response, age, suspected PARK2/PRKN, the external specialist input and the genuine later dyskinesia/behavioural risks without using later outcomes as hindsight.

### 2. Prospective review rule

Given that the record contemplated potentially substantial levodopa benefit and possible future regret from delay, what clinical findings should reasonably have triggered reconsideration of the original strategy?

### 3. Integrated foot question

Was parallel Neurology/Podiatry management reasonable given genuine structural foot pathology and the Parkinsonian/dystonic signs?

If so, what ongoing neurological review was appropriate while the relationship remained “Not clear”?

### 4. Prior Podiatry history

What significance, if any, should have been attached to the neurologist's knowledge that the patient had already undergone chiropody/Podiatry treatment since 2009–10 when Podiatry became the practical destination of the unresolved foot problem?

# B. Day 8 / discharge questions

### 5. The bedside challenge

If the patient's pre-discharge account is independently corroborated, did it amount to a clinically material challenge to the sufficiency of closure even if the Parkinsonian diagnosis itself remained persuasive?

### 6. What should have happened next?

If discharge remained appropriate after such a challenge, what findings, reasoning or safeguards would ordinarily make the decision defensible?

Would further integrated assessment, continued observation, movement-disorders input or another step have been reasonable alternatives even if not mandatory?

### 7. MRI and closure threshold

What did the MRI contribute to the decision that inpatient investigation was sufficient, and when was it reviewed relative to the bedside challenge and final discharge decision?

### 8. Documentation standard

What should an unusually detailed patient-facing discharge account ordinarily record when a patient understands a treatment rationale but disputes whether a major unresolved symptom makes discharge sufficient?

### 9. Understanding versus agreement

What can the phrase “good understanding” properly establish? Can it safely be used as evidence of agreement or acceptance of the wider management plan without preserving a material disagreement about closure?

# D. 2017–2023 re-evaluation questions

### 13. Procyclidine withdrawal / rechallenge

What clinical significance should reasonably have been assigned to deterioration in gait, restlessness and the feet during withdrawal, including the later clinician-documented rapid return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon?

Did that evidence make the foot/lower-limb syndrome sufficiently medication-state sensitive to require the original relationship to be revised or formally tested?

### 14. External specialist opinion

How should Healy's probable PARK2 assessment, low-dose dopamine-replacement model and warning against over-use be integrated with the local policy of prolonged restraint?

Did his response affirm prolonged non-use, cautious use or simply leave timing to the local team?

### 15. Treatment risk

Once patient-specific dyskinesia/chorea and behavioural effects emerged, did the reasonable model become one of a narrow therapeutic window in which both over-treatment and under-coverage required attention?

### 16. 2021 substitution

When procyclidine was removed and Sinemet introduced/increased, should adequacy have been tested specifically against the foot/lower-limb symptom domain that had shown prior medication-state sensitivity?

### 17. 2022 morning pattern

Did the reproducible 20–25 minute morning foot “warm-up” require structured assessment of medication timing, overnight coverage, rigidity/dystonia, structural stiffness or mixed mechanisms?

### 18. Later-2023 OFF threshold

Once Neurology itself described pain and shuffling as OFF-related / undertreated, what reassessment should reasonably have followed concerning dose timing, interval, consistency, food/absorption, formulation, evening/overnight coverage and specialist input?

Was this the point at which earlier evidence should have been reassembled rather than handled as separate episodes?

# E. Mechanism questions

### 19. What was actually medication-state sensitive?

Which components of the foot/lower-limb syndrome were probably modified by neurological medication state, and which remained structural, neuropathic-type, mechanical, dystonic/postural or otherwise mediated?

### 20. Pain generation versus modulation

Did dopamine-related treatment alter the generator of pain, the motor/postural conditions producing or loading pain, central pain modulation, or a combination?

# I. Breach and causation

### 29. Breach

At any identified date or period, did care fall below the standard of a reasonably competent neurologist or other relevant clinician?

For every breach identified, state:

- the date or period;
- evidence available then;
- reasonable action required;
- strongest defence evidence; and
- why the conclusion does not depend on hindsight.

### 30. Causation

For every breach, what consequence probably followed from that breach rather than the underlying disorder, structural foot disease, mixed pain mechanisms, treatment toxicity or ordinary progression?

### 31. Counterfactual treatment

If an earlier reasonable intervention is identified, what would probably have been done, would it probably have been tolerated, and would it probably have reduced pain, disability or functional loss to a material degree?

### 32. Period of loss

What is the defensible period of avoidable harm?

A broad 2017-to-2025 counterfactual requires more proof than a narrower period beginning when medication-state dependence, OFF-related pain or crisis severity became clinically explicit.

## Testing discipline

The record should not be resolved by choosing the most coherent story and then fitting every fact inside it.

For every material conclusion, identify whether it rests on:

- contemporaneous source evidence;
- later factual evidence;
- patient recollection;
- retrospective clinician explanation;
- clinical inference;
- standard-of-care opinion; or
- counterfactual causation opinion.

The strongest contrary evidence should be stated beside the conclusion it qualifies.
