# What the record shows

## Purpose

This page gives the legal team a compact documentary floor. It identifies propositions that can be traced to the complete record without requiring adoption of the patient's theory.

`CLIN-` references are stable addresses into `complete-record.md`. In the web reader they are automatically linked to the relevant location.

---

## 1. The presenting problem was integrated

The June 2017 urgent GP referral combined bilateral foot problems, abnormal gait, muscle tension, difficulty initiating movement, increased tone, brisk reflexes, toe clawing and progressive deterioration in one neurological referral: `CLIN-000847–CLIN-000884`.

The record does not support a starting assumption that the feet arrived as a wholly separate problem already adjudicated outside Neurology.

---

## 2. Structural foot pathology was genuine

The patient had real corns/calluses, deformity, clawing, pressure lesions and other podiatric findings. Podiatry involvement was therefore clinically intelligible and sometimes beneficial.

Any case theory that depends on saying the feet were “neurological all along” overstates the record.

---

## 3. The neurological presentation was also striking

Neurology documented rigidity, gross bradykinesia, abnormal gait and a lower-limb posture described in dystonic terms. The patient was exceptionally young for the presentation. Recessive genetic Parkinsonism including PARK2/PRKN was considered.

The relationship between the foot/leg domain and the neurological syndrome was not finally resolved. The discharge account described it as **“Not clear.”**

---

## 4. Treatment restraint was deliberate and its trade-off was acknowledged

The July 2017 account records deliberate caution with levodopa/dopamine agonists and the intention to use other symptomatic approaches first: `CLIN-001153–CLIN-001180`, `CLIN-001228–CLIN-001229`.

It also records the possibility that levodopa might work very well and that, in time, the clinicians might regret not starting it earlier.

This means the record itself identifies both the rationale for caution and the possible cost of excessive delay.

---

## 5. The treating neurologist knew the patient had years of prior foot treatment

The neurological history records chiropody/Podiatry treatment for the foot problem since approximately **2009–10**.

The further proposition that the patient therefore had a good practical understanding of what Podiatry could achieve is the patient's inference from that lived experience, not an express clinician statement.

---

## 6. The 2017 discharge account is unusually detailed and patient-facing

The document says it was intended in part for the patient, to consolidate discussions, assist debriefing and provide a clear plan.

It repeatedly describes the patient as intelligent, insightful, able to retain information and able to question appropriately. It records a **“good understanding”** of the treatment rationale.

The patient's reported pre-discharge challenge concerning the unresolved feet is not identifiable in that account.

That absence does not prove the challenge occurred or that any omission was deliberate. It defines a factual and documentary question requiring witness and version-history evidence.

---

## 7. The document was supplemented after physical discharge

The patient was discharged on 11 July. The DaTscan on 13 July was abnormal and was incorporated into the specialist correspondence before downstream receipt.

The scan strengthened Parkinsonism. It did not answer the mixed structural/neurological foot question.

This makes the lifecycle of the document relevant to what can fairly be inferred from its final form.

---

## 8. External specialist review supported probable PARK2 and genuine treatment caution

The treating neurologist sought advice from Professor Daniel Healy and expressly asked about genetics and whether to continue holding levodopa back: `CLIN-001720–CLIN-001730`.

Healy considered the patient likely to have PARK2, recommended testing and described the benefit/dyskinesia balance associated with dopamine replacement: `CLIN-001742–CLIN-001749`.

This is substantial defence evidence and also shows that the local treatment policy was consciously exposed to external review.

---

## 9. Neurological medication state later changed the foot/lower-limb domain

During procyclidine withdrawal, contemporaneous material recorded deterioration in tension, gait and the feet. The treating neurologist later recorded rapid return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon during withdrawal: `CLIN-001770–CLIN-001834`, `CLIN-001890–CLIN-001919`.

This does not establish a single neurological pain generator. It establishes clinically relevant modifiability.

---

## 10. Treatment toxicity was real

Dyskinesia/chorea, behavioural concerns and other treatment limitations became patient-specific rather than theoretical: `CLIN-002114–CLIN-002158`.

The case therefore cannot fairly be reduced to a simple allegation that more dopaminergic treatment should always have been given.

---

## 11. Later care genuinely reconsidered some foot/lower-limb phenomena

The record includes a foot movement described as Parkinsonian, later OFF-related pain/shuffling, investigation of alternative explanations and later genetic reconstruction.

Any claim that clinicians “never reconsidered anything” is inconsistent with the record.

The narrower question is whether individual acts of reinterpretation were ever assembled into a full reconsideration of the longstanding integrated foot problem.

---

## 12. The 2023 record contains an explicit crisis marker

The urgent GP referral records severe bilateral burning foot/lower-leg pain, says it could be extreme and **“causing death wish”**, records the GP's concern and asks for review before “we get into trouble here”: `CLIN-002637–CLIN-002641`.

The Parkinson's nurse then records debilitating bilateral pain and no quality of life despite pregabalin: `CLIN-002675–CLIN-002684`.

---

## 13. Later 2023 Neurology linked pain and shuffling to OFF state

A Movement Disorders nurse described the patient as appearing undertreated and stated that the leg pain and shuffling sounded OFF-related: `CLIN-002573–CLIN-002581`.

Treatment was adjusted, while dyskinesia risk remained relevant.

---

## 14. The 2025 record contains a second crisis marker and explicit medication relief

The treating neurologist records “murder in the feet”, the first approximately 150 footsteps as “torture”, and the patient's phrase “I won't live with it.” The same note records relief once Parkinson's medication takes effect: `CLIN-002777–CLIN-002800`.

Sinemet CR was introduced to extend overnight coverage. The later record describes profound relief: `CLIN-003243–CLIN-003269`.

---

## 15. The complete record contains no safeguarding response corresponding to the 2023 and 2025 crisis statements

No suicide-risk assessment, crisis plan, psychiatric/mental-health referral, safeguarding escalation or equivalent response has been identified in the complete record following those entries.

The legal and clinical significance of that absence is for the applicable standard to determine.

---

## 16. Genetics later confirmed PRKN/Parkin disease causing parkinsonism and dystonia

St Vincent's records in February 2026 that the patient's parkinsonism and dystonia are due to compound heterozygous mutations in the Parkin gene: `CLIN-003220–CLIN-003229`.

This confirms etiology later. It is not hindsight proof of what should have been concluded in 2017.

---

## 17. The record contains both support and resistance to the claimant's theory

A legally useful reading must preserve both:

- evidence that the foot/lower-limb state was repeatedly neurologically modifiable; and
- evidence of genuine structural disease, mixed pain mechanisms and serious treatment limits.

That coexistence is not a defect in the record. It is the clinical problem that an independent expert has to resolve.
