# Safeguarding

## Why this issue is central to the patient's decision

The patient has decided to pursue the case because the longstanding foot-pain problem eventually entered the clinical record in language concerning whether he could continue living with it, while the complete record contains no corresponding safeguarding response.

That is the patient's reason for taking the case.

It is not itself proof of breach. The task is to identify the standards that applied and compare the documented response with those standards.

---

## March 2023 — urgent GP referral

The National General Referral Form records severe bilateral **burning feet and lower legs and pain** and marks the referral urgent.

The GP records:

> **“Says extreme at time and causing death wish.”**

and later:

> **“I am worried about him…”**

followed by:

> **“Despite his facade, I don't think he is doing well. I'd be grateful for your review before we get into trouble here.”**

Key complete-record anchors are `CLIN-002637–CLIN-002641`.

This is not generic distress detached from the clinical problem. The language appears inside an urgent referral about severe bilateral foot and lower-leg pain.

---

## March 2023 — Parkinson's nurse communication

The nurse records ongoing possible neuropathic pain in both feet/legs despite pregabalin and states:

> **“Despite the Lyrica he states he is having debilitating pain and has no quality of life worsening over the last number of months.”**

See `CLIN-002675–CLIN-002684`.

The communication sought consultant recommendation/review.

---

## March 2025 — treating neurologist's own note

The neurologist later records the feet, mainly the left, as the main problem. The note includes:

- **“murder in the feet”**;
- the first approximately 150 footsteps as **“torture”**; and
- **“I won't live with it”** as the patient's way of putting words on the situation.

The same note also records that once Parkinson's medication hits his system there is relief in the feelings in the feet, and that the patient knows the feet feel better from a pain perspective once Sinemet takes effect.

See `CLIN-002777–CLIN-002800`.

The neurologist responded therapeutically by exploring and introducing nocturnal Sinemet CR because of the severity of the morning feet.

This matters because the strongest criticism is **not** that the foot complaint was literally ignored in 2025.

The question is whether therapeutic adjustment was sufficient given the accompanying life-threatening language and whether a separate safeguarding / risk-management response was required.

---

## What the complete record does not show

No corresponding response has been identified in the complete record in the form of:

- a documented suicide-risk assessment;
- a mental-health or psychiatric referral prompted by the statements;
- a crisis or safety plan;
- a safeguarding escalation;
- explicit safety-netting directed to risk of self-harm; or
- an equivalent documented risk-management response.

That is an absence in the available record. Its legal significance depends on the completeness of the record and the standard that applied.

## Questions requiring independent assessment

1. What risk-assessment or safeguarding standard applied to the March 2023 urgent referral?
2. What significance should a reasonable clinician have attached to pain described as “causing death wish” together with the GP's expressed concern?
3. What response should ordinarily have been documented or arranged?
4. What risk-management standard applied when the treating neurologist personally recorded “I won't live with it” in March 2025?
5. Does therapeutic adjustment of the Parkinson's medication answer the safeguarding question, or were the treatment and safety issues analytically separate?
6. What weight should be given to the absence of a safeguarding response in the complete record?
7. Is there evidence outside the current corpus of a risk assessment, referral, crisis plan or safety-netting that has not yet been obtained?
8. If a departure is identified, what harm did it probably cause or materially contribute to?

## The narrow formulation

The repository does not need to say that clinicians “caused suicidality” or knowingly ignored a suicidal patient.

The documentary proposition is sufficient:

> **The complete record contains two separate episodes in which severe foot pain is documented in language raising an obvious risk-to-life concern—“causing death wish” in 2023 and “I won't live with it” in 2025—and no corresponding safeguarding response has been identified in the complete record.**

By the second episode, neurological modifiability of the foot/lower-limb state was not an unknown proposition. The treating clinician himself recorded medication-related relief and altered overnight dopaminergic coverage.

The question is what the applicable standards required in those circumstances.
