### Central clinical issue

The central factual issue concerns what occurred, and what was documented, at the point of discharge from Galway University Hospital in July 2017.

I had entered hospital following an urgent GP referral in which gait, feet, muscle tension, movement initiation, toe clawing and neurological deterioration formed part of one unresolved presentation. Neurology strongly identified Parkinsonism, but the relationship between the neurological presentation and the leg/foot symptoms was recorded as “Not clear”. A Podiatry pathway nevertheless became operative.

Immediately before discharge, I recall expressly challenging the treating neurologist because the disabling foot problem remained unresolved. Four medical students were present. One went to obtain the consultant. During that discussion I used a distinctive NCT/bald-tyres analogy to explain why identifying one diagnosis did not, in my view, establish that the presenting problem had been adequately dealt with.

I appreciate that my recollection requires independent evidential assessment. The important documentary fact is that I have been unable to identify that challenge in the extensive patient-facing discharge correspondence.

Its absence potentially matters because the discharge documentation was expressly intended to consolidate the discussions that had taken place and provide a clear plan. It records me as highly insightful, able to question appropriately and having a “good understanding” of the treatment rationale. It therefore preserves evidence of my understanding and participation while apparently not preserving my challenge to whether the unresolved presenting problem had adequately been dealt with.

The discharge documentation was also subsequently supplemented after physical discharge with the 13 July DaTscan result. I therefore ask that its drafting history, amendment/version history and the source material from which the final account was compiled be examined rather than treating the final document as necessarily contemporaneous and complete in every respect.

### Why the missing-document issue is now important

This is no longer simply a question arising from my recollection.

In July 2025 I exercised my Article 15 GDPR right specifically to seek personal data and institutional documentation relating to the July 2017 bedside teaching interaction, including records concerning student involvement, ward rounds, supervisory/educational documentation and trainee assignments.

Galway University Hospitals responded on 5 August 2025. The response stated that it had been decided to “refuse” access to some records, but then stated in relation to several categories that the HSE did not hold personal data responsive to the request. It also referred to clinical documentation for 10–11 July **2015**, although my request concerned the July **2017** admission.

I do not suggest that those features prove that undisclosed records exist. They leave unresolved, however, what searches were actually undertaken, which repositories were examined, whether any responsive material was identified but withheld, and whether the 2017 encounter was searched under the correct admission/date parameters.

That issue is now before the DPC as part of complaint C-25-7-2074.

There is a related record-integrity issue. My June 2025 Article 16 request sought **rectification and annotation** of specified clinical material. GUH's eventual decision described the application at its outset as a request for **“erasure”**, while subsequently reproducing the actual rectification and annotation requests. I do not rely on that discrepancy as establishing clinical negligence. I raise it because it reinforces the need for independent examination of how my requests, and the underlying clinical-record issues, were characterised and processed.

On 4 August 2025, while the accuracy issue remained unresolved and a SIMT/internal review was contemplated, I also expressly invoked Article 18 restriction of processing in relation to the disputed material. I specifically requested identification of the data sources and versions to be used, an audit trail for extracts, and preservation of the relevant material.

The DPC Early Resolution Unit has since identified the Article 15, Article 16 and Article 18 aspects of the complaint and, on 10 August 2026, confirmed that the issues and information concerning HSE/Galway University Hospitals are **under assessment**.

I appreciate that the DPC process is distinct from any clinical-negligence claim and that no finding has been made by the Commission. Its relevance for present purposes is narrower: the completeness, provenance, search history and processing of the documentary record concerning the 2017 episode remain live and unresolved matters.

### Proposed negligence question

Against that background, my proposed litigation question is not simply whether the Parkinsonism diagnosis was wrong.

It is whether reasonable neurological care required the unresolved foot/neurological relationship to be integrated or formally reconsidered when the sufficiency of the proposed management was challenged; whether the discharge decision and subsequent management adequately addressed that unresolved presentation; and, if not, whether the failure caused or materially contributed to prolonged avoidable pain and persistence of an inappropriate management architecture.

The subsequent clinical history is relevant principally to that expert question.

Neurological medication later materially affected the feet. Withdrawal of procyclidine produced rapid return of a foot phenomenon documented by the treating neurologist. Later records described OFF-related pain. Sinemet CR produced profound relief in 2025, and PRKN testing ultimately established Parkin-related Parkinsonism and dystonia.

I do not rely upon those later developments simply as hindsight proof that the care provided in 2017 was negligent. I rely upon them as evidence potentially capable of identifying a neurological relationship which an independent expert should determine ought reasonably to have been investigated, integrated or reconsidered at particular stages.

### Subsequent reliance on the 2017 documentary account

In March 2025 I formally complained of diagnostic anchoring, failure to reassess and prolonged preventable pain.

In responding, the treating neurologist relied substantially upon the longitudinal correspondence, explaining that its purpose was to create clear documentation of how decisions were made, when and by whom. He referred specifically to the 2017 account that I had a “good understanding” of the treatment rationale and expressed the hope that the contemporaneous letters and his later account would provide the answers I was seeking.

That gives rise to what I regard as the central evidential question requiring independent advice:

**Can the documentary history safely be relied upon as demonstrating adequate consideration and communication of the original problem if a material patient challenge to the sufficiency of the discharge plan was omitted from the foundational account, and the completeness and provenance of the surrounding record remain unresolved?**

I am not asking that the answer be assumed. I am asking that it be investigated.
