## What the case does not need in order to be investigated

The central clinical-negligence inquiry does **not** need proof that:

- every foot symptom had one neurological cause;
- structural foot disease was unreal;
- Podiatry was inappropriate;
- treatment toxicity was unreal;
- the patient was right about the mechanism in 2017;
- the Day-8 protest was deliberately suppressed;
- clinicians acted in bad faith;
- later clinicians never reconsidered anything; or
- 2026 genetics was knowable in 2017.

---

## What would materially strengthen or weaken the case

### Could materially strengthen

- independent corroboration of the Day-8 bedside challenge;
- discharge-document draft/version/audit history showing the state of the document before and after the encounter;
- evidence that no appropriate safeguarding response exists outside the current corpus;
- independent movement-disorders opinion identifying a specific reassessment threshold and breach;
- expert causation evidence identifying a reasonable, tolerable alternative that probably would have reduced harm; and
- evidence that later care materially relied on inherited historical propositions without adequate re-testing where re-testing was clinically required.

### Could materially weaken

- evidence that the Day-8 encounter did not occur materially as remembered;
- contemporaneous documentation showing explicit integrated reconsideration before discharge;
- later records showing a safeguarding response not presently in the complete record;
- expert opinion that continued mixed Neurology/Podiatry management and the timing/formulation strategy remained within a reasonable range throughout;
- evidence that earlier alternative treatment would probably not have been tolerated or would not have changed the pain/disability; and
- evidence that later historical reconstruction occurred more fully than the current corpus reveals.

## The narrowest durable formulation

The strongest documentary proposition is not that the clinicians were wrong from the beginning.

It is:

> **A serious and mixed presenting problem remained partly unresolved in 2017. Later patient-specific evidence repeatedly showed neurological modulation of the foot/lower-limb domain while structural pathology and treatment toxicity remained real. The record therefore requires independent assessment of when, if ever, reasonable care required a more integrated reassessment, what should have followed, and whether any failure caused harm.**

The safeguarding branch adds a separate later question:

> **When severe foot pain was documented in 2023 and 2025 using language concerning continued life, what risk-management response did the applicable standards require, and where is that response in the record?**
