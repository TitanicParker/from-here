# Evidence gaps and preservation

## Purpose

Some of the most important remaining questions are capable of factual resolution. This page identifies the evidence that may answer them and should therefore be considered for immediate preservation or discovery.

---

## 1. The Day-8 bedside teaching encounter

### Present position

The encounter is a later, detailed patient account. Four medical students are said to have been present; one allegedly went to obtain the neurologist.

### Evidence capable of resolving it

- student cohort / rotation lists;
- clinical placement allocations;
- attendance records;
- ward or service assignment;
- tutor / consultant assignment;
- teaching schedules;
- student logbooks or learning records;
- clinical-placement administration records;
- hospital education-office records;
- duty/team records capable of identifying the supervising unit;
- nursing or ward notes;
- contemporaneous patient communications; and
- neutral witness interviews if students are identified.

### Why the distinctive analogy matters

The NCT / bald-tyres analogy may operate as a useful memory discriminator. Former students should not be fed the distinctive detail before being asked what, if anything, they independently remember.

1. MRI requested;

6. discharge decision finalised;

## 4. LTI chronology and primary form

The record refers to Long Term Illness paperwork around the admission.

The original signed/stamped form, exact date, declaration, signatories and administrative meaning should be preserved where available.

The patient's participation in administrative paperwork should not be treated as agreement with every element of the clinical plan without examining what the form actually represented.

## 5. Completeness of the clinical archive

Absence-based claims depend on knowing what the archive contains and what categories are missing.

The source inventory should identify whether relevant nursing, MDT, discharge-planning, ward-round, teaching, trainee or risk-assessment material exists, existed and was later destroyed, or was never created.

Retention and deletion policies should be preserved.

## 6. Safeguarding records

Because the complete record contains no identified safeguarding response to the 2023 and 2025 crisis statements, targeted preservation/search should include:

- suicide-risk or self-harm assessments;
- psychiatric / mental-health referrals;
- crisis-team communications;
- safety plans;
- safeguarding referrals;
- telephone advice or undocumented-system notes;
- GP/Neurology communications concerning risk; and
- local policies identifying where such material would ordinarily be recorded.

## 7. Article 15 search history and institutional searches

The July/August 2025 access process sought records concerning the bedside teaching interaction and student involvement.

Relevant preservation may include:

- systems/repositories searched;
- search terms and date parameters used;
- personnel conducting searches;
- results logs;
- records identified but considered outside scope;
- records withheld or not held;
- retention/deletion information; and
- clarification of the reference in the response to 10–11 July 2015 when the request concerned July 2017.

The purpose is not to presume undisclosed records exist. It is to establish what search was actually performed.

---

## 8. SIMT / internal-review source control

Where an internal review was contemplated while the accuracy dispute remained live, preserve:

- the source documents supplied to reviewers;
- versions of disputed records;
- extracts created for review;
- audit trail for those extracts;
- reviewer instructions; and
- any restrictions or annotations applied to the disputed data.

This is relevant to documentary provenance and should remain separate from the clinical-negligence merits unless an independent legal basis connects them.

---

## 9. Later reliance on the 2017 account

Where later clinicians or the 2025 complaint response rely on earlier correspondence, identify:

- explicit quotations;
- textual carry-forward;
- source references;
- dates of access to prior notes where available; and
- any independent assessment that supports or contradicts the inherited proposition.

This can help distinguish later independent reasoning from later repetition of an earlier synthesis without assuming either in advance.

---

## Immediate preservation principle

The evidence most likely to become harder to obtain with time is not necessarily the medical diagnosis itself. It is the **document lifecycle and witness infrastructure** surrounding a nine-year-old admission.

Those records should be considered urgently in light of limitation and ordinary retention/deletion processes.
