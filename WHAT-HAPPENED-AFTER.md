# What happened afterwards

## Purpose

The later record matters because it repeatedly supplied new information about the feet, lower limbs, medication state, treatment benefit and treatment risk.

The correct question is not whether every later event proves the 2017 formulation wrong. It is:

> **What did each later event require clinicians reasonably to reconsider at the time it occurred?**

The record contains genuine reconsideration. It also contains a recurring question about whether local treatment changes were accompanied by an integrated re-examination of the longstanding foot problem.

---

## 2017 — specialist PARK2 opinion

The treating neurologist sought external advice and expressly put the proposed levodopa-restraint policy to Professor Daniel Healy: `CLIN-001720–CLIN-001730`.

Healy considered the patient likely to have PARK2, recommended genetic testing, noted dramatic response to treatment and described the characteristic difficulty of obtaining benefit from relatively low dopamine replacement without over-treatment and dyskinesia: `CLIN-001742–CLIN-001749`.

This is important counterevidence against any claim that the case was simply ignored or that caution was invented later.

It also created an expert question about how a low-dose / avoid-over-use model should be compared with a policy of leaving conventional dopamine replacement off for as long as possible.

---

## 2017–2018 — medication withdrawal changes the disputed domain

Procyclidine produced marked functional improvement. During planned withdrawal, contemporaneous evidence recorded increasing tension, restless feet and deterioration of gait: `CLIN-001770–CLIN-001834`.

In 2018, the treating neurologist recorded the earlier episode more specifically: the first thing noticed changing rapidly was the return of the outside-left-foot/corn phenomenon: `CLIN-001890–CLIN-001919`.

This did **not** prove that a corn was neurological or that all foot pain was dystonic.

It established a narrower patient-specific fact capable of clinical significance:

> **At least part of the disputed foot/lower-limb state was modifiable by neurological medication state.**

The question is what that new evidence did to the original “Not clear” relationship.

---

## 2018–2019 — treatment risk becomes real

The patient later developed genuine dyskinesia/chorea and behavioural or impulsivity concerns with stronger dopaminergic treatment. The clinician described management as an extremely difficult “tight rope”: `CLIN-002114–CLIN-002158`.

This materially strengthens the defence of continued caution.

It also means the later problem cannot sensibly be framed as simply “too little dopamine.” The patient could experience harm at treatment peaks and disability or pain when coverage was inadequate or badly timed.

The more plausible management problem became a **narrow therapeutic window** involving dose, timing, interval, formulation and duration as well as total exposure.

---

## 2020 — a foot phenomenon is described as Parkinsonian

A foot-drop-like “throw” was later described by Neurology as **“actually parkinsonian”** in the context of normal power.

This is a genuine act of clinical reinterpretation. It matters because it shows that later clinicians did not merely repeat the original account.

The remaining question is whether a phenomenon-specific reinterpretation was ever used to reconstruct the broader historical relationship among structural feet, pain, dystonia/Parkinsonism and the original Podiatry allocation.

---

## 2021 — levodopa introduced and procyclidine removed

Sinemet was introduced in 2021 when the patient was considered undertreated. Procyclidine was then removed in part because of ocular pressure concerns and Sinemet was increased: `CLIN-002330–CLIN-002353`.

That substitution created a testable question because the earlier drug had already shown that the foot/lower-limb domain changed with neurological medication state.

> **Known foot medication-state response → procyclidine removed → Sinemet substituted → was the foot-specific adequacy of the substitution actually tested?**

The surviving record does not clearly show a structured foot-specific substitution assessment. Documentary silence is not proof that no clinical thought occurred.

---

## 2022 — morning foot “warm-up”

The record documented the feet taking approximately 20–25 minutes to “warm up” on many mornings, while structural soreness from corns/calluses also remained relevant: `CLIN-002375–CLIN-002386`.

At the time this could reasonably have been a non-specific clue.

Its evidential weight changes when read beside earlier withdrawal evidence and later OFF-state observations.

The expert question is whether this pattern should have triggered structured attention to morning dose timing, overnight coverage, rigidity/dystonia, structural stiffness or mixed mechanisms.

---

## March 2023 — severe pain and crisis language

The urgent GP referral records severe bilateral burning foot and lower-leg pain, states that it could be extreme and **“causing death wish”**, and says:

> **“I am worried about him … I'd be grateful for your review before we get into trouble here.”**

Key anchors include `CLIN-002637–CLIN-002641`.

The Parkinson's nurse then recorded ongoing bilateral foot/leg pain despite pregabalin and stated that the patient described debilitating pain and no quality of life: `CLIN-002675–CLIN-002684`.

This episode also contains contrary evidence: a report of a period off Parkinson medication without a perceived general Parkinson difference. That evidence should not be erased merely because later OFF-state observations point in another direction.

The proper response to conflicting observations may itself be an expert question about structured testing rather than choosing one anecdotal state as decisive.

---

## Later 2023 — Neurology describes pain and shuffling as OFF-related

Later in 2023 the record documented severe foot pain, reduced mobility and shuffling that were worse in the morning and later in the day if Sinemet had not been taken. A Movement Disorders nurse wrote that it sounded as though the Parkinson's was undertreated and the **leg pain and shuffling were OFF related**: `CLIN-002573–CLIN-002581`.

Treatment was adjusted.

This is one of the strongest pre-2025 points at which the record itself gave a neurological interpretation to pain/motor deterioration in the disputed domain.

The question is whether that should have triggered an integrated historical reassessment of the earlier foot problem, or whether it was clinically reasonable to manage it as a current OFF phenomenon within a still-mixed model.

---

## 2023–2024 — pregabalin benefit and persistent motor/postural features

Pregabalin materially improved pain. Toe curling/clawing and other functional or postural features could nevertheless persist.

This argues strongly against a single-cause story.

It is compatible with a mixed syndrome in which structural pathology, pain processing, dystonia/posture, loading and dopaminergic medication state all contribute in different proportions at different times.

The existence of mixed mechanisms does not remove the need to test whether individual components are treatable or whether the integrated model remains adequate.

---

## March 2025 — severe morning feet and medication relief

The treating neurologist recorded:

- morning foot pain described as **“murder in the feet”**;
- the first approximately 150 footsteps as **“torture”**;
- the patient's phrase **“I won't live with it”**;
- relief in the feet once Parkinson's medication entered the system; and
- a long overnight gap in immediate-release Sinemet coverage.

Key anchors include `CLIN-002777–CLIN-002800`.

Sinemet CR was introduced to extend overnight coverage.

The later complaint response described the resulting benefit as **“profound relief”**: `CLIN-003243–CLIN-003269`.

This is strong evidence about the then-current severe morning state. It is not automatic proof of the same mechanism in every earlier year.

It does, however, give later weight to the significance of timing and duration of dopaminergic coverage.

---

## 2026 — PRKN/Parkin confirmation

St Vincent's later confirmed that:

> **“His Parkinsonism and dystonia are due to compound heterozygous mutations in the Parkin gene.”**

See `CLIN-003220–CLIN-003229`.

The result confirms the eventual etiological diagnosis and validates the seriousness of the PARK2/PRKN hypothesis raised prospectively in 2017.

It must not be back-projected as genetic certainty available to earlier clinicians, and it does not classify every historical foot lesion or pain episode as neurological.

---

## The longitudinal question

The later record is best approached as two streams that both need to be carried forward:

### Evidence of treatment benefit / neurological modifiability

- dramatic early response;
- deterioration during withdrawal including gait and feet;
- rapid focal foot return recorded later;
- later Parkinsonian interpretation of a foot phenomenon;
- OFF-related pain and shuffling;
- morning relief when medication took effect; and
- profound improvement after overnight coverage was extended.

### Evidence of treatment risk / competing mechanisms

- real structural foot pathology;
- mechanical treatment effects;
- pregabalin benefit;
- visual/ocular procyclidine problems;
- dyskinesia/chorea;
- behavioural or impulse-control concerns; and
- contradictory observations during medication interruption.

A fair expert review has to explain both streams.

The central question is whether the clinical model was repeatedly re-tested as those streams accumulated, and at what point, if any, reasonable practice required more systematic integrated reassessment or renewed specialist input.
