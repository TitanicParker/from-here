# Counterevidence and limits

## Purpose

The case should be capable of surviving its strongest contrary evidence. This page preserves material that prevents an over-simple claimant narrative.

---

## Structural foot disease was real

Podiatry documented callus, clawing, pressure lesions, deformity and biomechanical abnormalities. Mechanical treatment sometimes helped.

The record cannot honestly be reduced to “the feet were neurological all along.”

---

## The patient already had an established Podiatry history

Foot treatment pre-dated the neurological admission by years. This supports the reasonableness of continued Podiatry involvement.

It also means that the legal/clinical question is not whether Podiatry should have existed, but whether the neurological relationship remained sufficiently open to reassessment alongside it.

---

## Pregabalin materially helped pain

Lyrica/pregabalin substantially reduced pain at points in the later history.

That is evidence consistent with neuropathic-type or mixed pain mechanisms. It does not etiologically prove that the entire syndrome was non-neurological.

---

## Treatment toxicity was genuine

Procyclidine caused visual/ocular concerns. Dopaminergic therapy produced dyskinesia/chorea and behavioural or impulse-control concerns.

These were real clinical constraints, not merely later rationalisations.

Any expert criticism must explain how a reasonable alternative would have managed those risks.

---

## Healy independently supported probable PARK2 and caution

The external specialist considered PARK2 probable and recognised the characteristic balance between benefit from relatively low dopamine replacement and dyskinesia if over-used.

This is important evidence that the unusual diagnosis and treatment difficulty were being taken seriously.

---

## Later clinicians genuinely reconsidered the case

The record contains later neurological reinterpretation of a foot movement, OFF-related pain/shuffling, consideration of OFF dystonia, investigation of alternative causes, treatment changes and eventual genetic reconstruction.

It would be inaccurate to say that later care was nothing but passive repetition of 2017.

The narrower question is whether those acts ever produced a sufficiently integrated historical reassessment of the original foot problem.

---

## MRI completion was a legitimate inpatient endpoint

The treating neurologist later explained that he had been keen to keep the patient in hospital until MRI could be obtained. The MRI was completed around 10 July.

This is relevant evidence that the team had an identifiable clinical reason for when the admission could end.

If the Day-8 challenge is corroborated, the issue becomes whether that patient-raised concern required a different or additional closure analysis.

---

## The 2023 record contains contradictory medication evidence

The March 2023 material includes a report of a period off Parkinson medication without the patient perceiving an obvious general Parkinson difference.

That observation does not erase earlier withdrawal evidence or later OFF-related pain. It is contrary evidence that an expert must reconcile.

---

## The DaTscan strongly supported Parkinsonism

The post-discharge DaTscan was abnormal and strengthened the Parkinsonian diagnosis.

It did not prove that the Podiatry pathway was wrong, that every foot symptom was neurological or that discharge was inappropriate.

---

## The 2026 genetic result cannot be back-projected

PRKN/Parkin confirmation gives strong later etiological clarity. It does not establish what a reasonable clinician was required to know years before the result existed.

---

## The protest remains uncorroborated

The patient's Day-8 account is detailed and specific but currently patient-sourced.

The core longitudinal clinical question does not have to depend on proving the protest. The protest matters especially to the contested-closure, patient-voice and documentary-omission branch.

Independent corroboration would materially change its evidential status.

---

## Omission does not prove intent

The absence of the reported protest from the discharge account does not establish deliberate concealment, bad faith or strategic omission.

The first task is factual: determine whether the event occurred, when the document was drafted, whether it remained open to amendment and what source/version history survives.

---

## Later benefit does not prove earlier negligence

The 2025 response to Sinemet CR is strong evidence that overnight dopaminergic coverage materially altered the then-current severe morning problem.

It does not automatically prove that the same treatment should have been prescribed years earlier, would have been tolerated, or would have prevented the intervening harm.

---

## Reassessment and causation are separate

Even if an expert concludes that additional assessment or re-synthesis should have occurred, it does not follow automatically that a different treatment would probably have succeeded or that all later loss is attributable to the omission.

Breach, counterfactual management and causation must be answered separately.

---

## Strongest reasonable defence account

A fair defence may say:

1. the patient genuinely had very-young-onset Parkinsonism, later genetically confirmed;
2. genuine structural foot disease independently justified Podiatry;
3. the neurological-foot relationship was mixed rather than simply missed;
4. conventional dopaminergic treatment carried unusual and later-proven risks;
5. Healy independently supported probable PARK2 and a cautious treatment model;
6. the team completed relevant inpatient investigation including MRI;
7. later pain responded to non-dopaminergic treatment as well as Parkinson medication;
8. later clinicians repeatedly reconsidered individual phenomena; and
9. treatment changes were constrained by a real narrow therapeutic window.

The claimant's case should not attempt to defeat that account rhetorically. It should identify which questions the defence explains and which remain unanswered when the entire record is examined.
